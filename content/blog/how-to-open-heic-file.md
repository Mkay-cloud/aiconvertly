You AirDropped or emailed yourself a photo from an iPhone, and now your computer refuses to open it. The filename ends in `.HEIC`, and Windows, or an old app, or a website upload form just shrugs. You're not doing anything wrong. This is one of the most common file-format headaches around, and the fastest fix takes about ten seconds: convert the photo to JPG in your browser.

## Why HEIC Won't Open Everywhere

HEIC stands for High Efficiency Image Container. Apple made it the default photo format on iPhones back in iOS 11 (2017), and it's still the default today on iPhone 7 and newer. The appeal is real: HEIC photos look just as good as JPEGs but take up roughly half the storage space, which matters when you're shooting thousands of photos on a phone with limited storage.

The catch is that HEIC is a much newer, less universally supported format than JPEG. Apple's own devices (iPhone, iPad, Mac) all handle it natively, and [Apple documents exactly which models support it](https://support.apple.com/en-us/116944). Plenty of other software, especially older or non-Apple programs, still doesn't know what to do with a `.HEIC` file, which is exactly why it opens fine on the phone that took it and nowhere else.

## On a Mac, iPad, or iPhone? You Don't Need to Do Anything

If you're opening the file on a Mac, iPad, or another iPhone, HEIC just works. It's Apple's own format, and Preview, Photos, and Quick Look all display it natively on any device on [Apple's supported-models list](https://support.apple.com/en-us/116944), going back as far as the iPhone 7 and iPad Air 3rd generation. If a HEIC file won't open on an Apple device that's on that list, the file itself is likely corrupted or incomplete, not a format problem, and converting it (as above) is the more reliable fix than troubleshooting the original file.

## Add Native HEIC Support to Windows

If you're on Windows 10 or 11 and you get HEIC photos regularly, it's worth teaching your PC to understand the format permanently instead of converting files one at a time.

1. Open the Microsoft Store and search for **HEIF Image Extensions**, or go straight to its [store listing](https://apps.microsoft.com/detail/9pmmsr1cgpwg). ![the Microsoft Store's HEIF Image Extensions listing page, with the Get option visible](/blog/how-to-open-heic-file-shot-01.svg)
2. Click **Get** (or **Install**) to add it. It's a free download from Microsoft.
3. If Windows also offers the companion **HEVC Video Extensions** at the same time, install that too. Some HEIC files use HEVC-based encoding internally, and without it you can end up with a generic file icon instead of a real thumbnail.
4. Once both are installed, HEIC photos will show real thumbnails in File Explorer and open directly in the Photos app, the same as a JPEG would, with no further steps.

This is the better option if HEIC keeps showing up in your downloads and email attachments and you're tired of dealing with it file by file. It's a one-time setup that fixes every future HEIC photo on that PC, not just the one in front of you right now.

## Stop Getting HEIC Files in the First Place

If it's *your* iPhone taking the photos and you'd rather avoid this problem going forward, you can tell it to shoot JPEG instead of HEIC:

1. Open **Settings** → **Camera** → **Formats**.
2. Choose **Most Compatible** instead of "High Efficiency."

New photos will save as JPEG, which opens everywhere without a second thought. The tradeoff is storage: JPEGs at the same quality take up roughly twice the space, so this is worth doing only if compatibility matters more to you than saving space on your phone. It only affects new photos, it won't convert anything you've already taken, and Apple's own support page covers the same toggle if you want to double-check the exact wording on your iOS version.
