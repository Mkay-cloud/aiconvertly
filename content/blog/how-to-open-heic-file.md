You AirDropped or emailed yourself a photo from an iPhone, and now your computer refuses to open it. The filename ends in `.HEIC`, and Windows — or an old app, or a website upload form — just shrugs. You're not doing anything wrong. This is one of the most common file-format headaches around, and it has a handful of reliable fixes, from a quick Windows setting to a fully software-free option that works on literally any device.

## What Is a HEIC File, Anyway?

HEIC stands for High Efficiency Image Container. Apple made it the default photo format on iPhones back in iOS 11 (2017), and it's still the default today. The appeal is real: HEIC photos look just as good as JPEGs but take up roughly half the storage space, which matters when you're shooting thousands of photos on a phone with limited storage.

The catch is that HEIC is a much newer, less universally supported format than JPEG. Apple's own devices — iPhone, iPad, Mac — all handle it natively. Plenty of other software, especially older or non-Apple programs, simply doesn't know what to do with it yet.

## Fix 1: On a Windows PC

If you're on Windows 10 or 11, the fix is usually a free download away. Open the Microsoft Store, search for **"HEIF Image Extensions,"** and install it (Windows may also prompt you to install the companion "HEVC Video Extensions" — install that too if it's offered). Once it's installed, HEIC photos will show thumbnails in File Explorer and open directly in the Photos app, just like a JPEG would.

This is the best option if you regularly receive HEIC photos and want your PC to simply understand the format going forward, rather than converting files one at a time.

## Fix 2: On a Mac or iPad

If you're opening the file on a Mac, iPad, or another iPhone, you don't need to do anything — HEIC is Apple's own format, and Preview, Photos, and Quick Look all display it natively. If a HEIC file won't open on an Apple device, the file itself is likely corrupted or incomplete, not a format issue.

## Fix 3: Change Your iPhone's Camera Format

If it's *your* iPhone taking the photos and you'd rather avoid this problem going forward, you can tell it to shoot JPEG instead of HEIC:

1. Open **Settings** → **Camera** → **Formats**
2. Choose **"Most Compatible"** instead of "High Efficiency"

New photos will save as JPEG, which opens everywhere without a second thought. The tradeoff is storage: JPEGs at the same quality take up roughly twice the space, so this is worth doing only if compatibility matters more to you than saving space on your phone.

This only affects new photos — it won't convert anything you've already taken.

## Fix 4: Convert It Online, No Software Needed

If you just need to open *this one photo* — right now, on whatever device you happen to be using — installing an extension or digging through camera settings is overkill. The simplest fix is to convert the HEIC file to JPEG in your browser: no software to install, no app to trust with your photos, and it works identically on Windows, Mac, Chromebook, or anything else with a web browser.

The conversion itself is quick: HEIC and JPEG both store a single compressed image, so turning one into the other doesn't lose any meaningful quality — you're just repackaging the same photo into a format nearly everything can read.

## Which Fix Should You Use?

If you get HEIC photos regularly, install the free Windows extension (Fix 1) and stop thinking about it. If it's a one-off photo you need to open right now, converting it online is faster than installing anything. And if you're the one taking the photos, switching your iPhone to "Most Compatible" mode means the people you send photos to never hit this problem in the first place.
